Nasdaq pre-market range sweep — weekly indicator review

October 26–30, 2026 | +10R

This week, we're once again reviewing how our NASDAQ Pre-Market Range Sweep indicator performed on real charts, using two types of signals: the main reversal signals after a liquidity sweep beyond the pre-market range, and the new Expansion signals we added just two weeks ago.

As usual, I'm using the 1-minute chart with the indicator's default settings. I haven't optimized or adjusted anything specifically for this past week.

How we count the results

We calculate results directly from the entry price provided by the indicator. We don't look back and search for a better entry on a pullback, an FVG, or any additional confirmation.

Even when price travels significantly further, we count each successful signal as only +1R, using a fixed 1:1 target relative to the stop used in the review.

This allows us to evaluate the signals under a consistent approach rather than measure the maximum possible profit from perfect trade management.

Monday, October 26

After a liquidity sweep beyond the pre-market range, the first BUY signal appeared around 29,161.

Price reached the 1:1 target and then continued moving higher. With active trade management, it would have been possible to capture significantly more, but according to our review rules, we record only +1R.

Later, the indicator generated an Expansion signal.

This signal also reached its 1:1 target. Whether using a tighter stop or placing the protective stop beyond the more distant extreme shown on the chart, the movement was sufficient to achieve our minimum 1:1 risk-to-reward ratio.

2 signals → 2 targets reached Monday's result: +2R

Tuesday, October 27

After liquidity was swept above the upper boundary of the pre-market range, the indicator generated a SELL signal around 29,469.

Price moved down to the target near 29,395, delivering +1R.

The market then returned inside the range, moved beyond its boundaries again, and generated an Expansion BUY signal.

The subsequent movement was much stronger than our minimum target. With active trade management, considerably more could have been captured, but once again, we record only +1R.

2 signals → 2 targets reached Tuesday's result: +2R

Wednesday, October 28

The most interesting and challenging day of the week

Wednesday was FOMC decision day. Personally, I prefer to avoid trading on days like this, especially immediately before the announcement and during the market's initial reaction.

But since we're reviewing the indicator's signals consistently, let's examine this day as well.

The first BUY: a long wait for the target

The first BUY signal appeared at approximately 10:05 AM New York time, around 29,728, following a liquidity sweep beyond the range boundary.

After the entry, price entered a prolonged consolidation. In the illustrated setup, the position remained open for several hours. It wasn't until around 2:30 PM New York time that the market finally reached the 1:1 target.

And this is where something particularly interesting happened.

An opposite Expansion SELL appeared

While the first BUY setup was still open, the indicator generated an opposite signal — Expansion SELL.

For a trader, this can be a difficult situation. Seeing a new SELL signal, you might decide that the bullish scenario is no longer valid, close the original BUY, and open a SELL position instead.

But what actually happened?

The Expansion SELL hit its stop-loss. The original BUY's protective stop was never touched, and that setup eventually reached its target.

Switching direction too quickly could therefore have meant missing the BUY target and taking a loss on the SELL.

It's a good example of why an opposite signal doesn't necessarily mean that an existing position should automatically be closed.

If your account conditions allow positions in opposite directions, you can technically consider both signals independently, while accounting for the combined risk in advance.

But that doesn't mean you must take every signal. Your risk limits and trading plan remain more important than the number of opportunities.

A third signal: Expansion BUY

At approximately 2:44 PM New York time, a third signal appeared — Expansion BUY.

After a pullback, the market moved sharply higher. Around 4:00 PM New York time, following the FOMC events, we saw a strong upward movement with sharp price spikes.

This third signal also reached its 1:1 target.

Despite the prolonged consolidation, opposite signals, and elevated volatility, our fixed-result model finished positive:

First BUY → +1R Expansion SELL → −1R Expansion BUY → +1R

3 signals → 2 targets reached → 1 stop-loss Wednesday's result: +1R

Wednesday reminds us that a successful signal doesn't necessarily work quickly, and the appearance of an opposite signal doesn't prove that the original scenario has stopped working.

Thursday, October 29

On Thursday, the indicator generated three signals.

The first SELL appeared after a liquidity sweep above the upper boundary of the pre-market range and reached its 1:1 target.

Later, an Expansion SELL appeared, followed by another Expansion SELL continuing the bearish movement.

Both Expansion signals reached their targets as well. Treating them as separate setups with partially overlapping holding periods, the movement was sufficient to deliver at least 1:1 for each illustrated entry and stop.

A particularly strong downward move began after 4:00 PM New York time. NASDAQ continued falling far beyond the initial targets.

With active trade management, the potential result was considerably higher, but we don't increase our recorded results after seeing what happened on the chart.

3 signals → 3 targets reached Thursday's result: +3R

Friday, October 30

On Friday, we once again saw a classic reversal setup.

After liquidity was swept beyond the pre-market range boundary, price returned inside the range, and the indicator generated a BUY signal.

The first signal reached its 1:1 target.

Later, an Expansion SELL appeared, followed by a strong continuation lower in NASDAQ.

This signal reached our minimum target and also offered room for further trade management: taking partial profits, moving the stop-loss to breakeven, or holding a portion of the position to capture the continuation.

Depending on the management approach, the result could have been significantly greater than +1R. But in this review, we don't claim the maximum chart movement as our own profit.

2 signals → 2 targets reached Friday's result: +2R

These statistics represent chart-based signal results, not the actual performance of a trading account. They don't include commissions, slippage, or execution conditions. Overlapping positions can also increase the combined risk.

What does this week show us?

The most important observation this week is that the Expansion signals added new entry opportunities alongside the original range-return signals.

Liquidity sweep → Reversal signal → Return toward the range Range breakout → Expansion signal → Continuation opportunity

At the same time, Wednesday demonstrated why every signal needs to be evaluated within a predefined trading plan.

An opposite signal is new information. It doesn't automatically invalidate an earlier setup whose protective stop remains untouched.

We also saw why the time needed to reach a target matters. A setup that takes several hours to resolve may not suit a trader who wants short intraday positions, even if it eventually reaches +1R.

I continue to use the indicator's default settings and share the results without adjusting them to fit historical price action.
