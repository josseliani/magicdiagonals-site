NASDAQ Pre-Market Range Sweep — Weekly Review, October 5–9, 2026

October 5–9, 2026Published October 11, 2026 by josseliani

Another week of reviewing how our NASDAQ Pre-Market Range Sweep indicator performed on the 1-minute chart.

But first, remember that untested level from Friday, October 2, which we left on our chart in last week's review?

Keep it in mind. Something interesting happened this week.

Monday, October 5

One SELL signal. The market continued moving higher, and our trade hit its stop-loss.

Result: −1R

Tuesday, October 6

One SELL signal, with entry already inside the pre-market range.

Price reached the range midpoint and moved slightly below it. We could take partial profit at the midpoint or close the trade at 1R.

Result: +1R

Wednesday, October 7

One BUY signal.

Price reached the range midpoint, where we could take partial profit, and then continued to the upper boundary.

Moving the stop to breakeven after 1R would not have stopped us out.

Result: up to +1.8R

Thursday, October 8

One SELL signal from the indicator at approximately 16:41.

The trade reached its 1R target.

But then something interesting happened.

Right above the pre-market range level, price formed a clear Head and Shoulders pattern.

At approximately 19:41, price broke below the neckline and continued falling.

And where did it go?

Straight to that untested level from Friday, October 2!

The level we had kept on our chart since the previous week.

Friday, October 9

Two BUY signals.

Both offered almost 2R.

Result: approximately +2R per trade.

What does this week show us?

First, the indicator gives us signals with relatively short stop-losses.

Even with a very simple approach — taking the signals, placing stops behind nearby local highs or lows, and looking for at least 1:1 risk/reward — we can achieve a positive result.

We've been seeing positive results from this approach in our weekly reviews for several weeks now.

Of course, we can also take partial profits, move stops to breakeven, or try to capture a larger move. That depends on our trading style.

But there's another important advantage.

The indicator doesn't just give us BUY and SELL signals. It also helps us see the market structure.

Previous ranges, liquidity levels, and untested boundaries remain visible on our charts.

Thursday was a perfect example.

The indicator gave us one SELL signal, which delivered 1R. It did not generate another signal for the Head and Shoulders pattern.

But we could see the structure forming around the pre-market range, the liquidity sweep above it, and the subsequent break and candle close below the lower range level.

Now we had a reason to consider another short trade, a structural reference for our stop-loss, and a potential target — the untested level from October 2.

And that's the important difference.

We couldn't simply sell NASDAQ every day, hoping that price would eventually return to that old level.

An untested level is not a trading signal or a guarantee.

Without a proper setup, where would we put our stop-loss? How much drawdown would we have to survive?

Instead, we keep those levels in mind and wait for the market to give us a reason to act.

On Thursday, that reason was the Head and Shoulders breakdown.

The indicator helped us identify the levels. Our trading experience helped us recognize the opportunity.

The weekly chart

Look at the 5-minute chart.

The untested October 2 level remained visible throughout the week, and Thursday's decline finally brought price back to it.

I've noticed over several recent weeks that Mondays often bring strong directional moves, while Wednesday or Thursday sometimes brings corrections toward earlier levels.

It's just an observation, not a rule or a reason to trade without confirmation.

Very simple indicator. Very simple strategy.
