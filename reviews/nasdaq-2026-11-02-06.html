NASDAQ Pre-Market Range Sweep — Weekly Review, November 2–6, 2026

November 2–6, 2026Week result: +7.1RPublished November 7, 2026 by josseliani

Another week of NASDAQ trading, and another interesting opportunity to see how the Pre-Market Range Sweep indicator performs under different market conditions.

This week brought clean range-to-range moves, Expansion signals, one stop-loss, and a particularly interesting countertrend setup during a powerful market selloff.

What makes this week worth reviewing is not just the final result. It is how the indicator responded to different situations — when it confirmed an entry, when it waited, and when the market moved too aggressively to meet its conditions.

Let's go through the week.

The week in numbers

  • 6 completed setups
  • 5 targets reached
  • 1 stop-loss

WEEKLY RESULT: +7.1R

Monday: +3.6R
Tuesday: No signals
Wednesday: +1R
Thursday: +1R
Friday: +1.5R

This educational review evaluates historical indicator signals using their illustrated entries, stop-losses and predefined targets. The R results represent theoretical setup outcomes, not personal trading profits or a record of executed trades.

Multiple Expansion signals appearing within the same setup zone are counted as one opportunity.

Friday's unresolved Expansion BUY signals are excluded from the completed weekly result and will be tracked separately.

Monday, November 2

Two SELL setups → +3.6R

Monday gave us two excellent examples of how the original range-sweep logic and the Expansion module can work during the same session.

Original SELL → +2.3R

Price swept the upper boundary of the pre-market range, returned inside, and confirmed a SELL setup.

From there, price travelled all the way to the lower boundary of the range.

Upper boundary sweep → Return inside → SELL confirmation → Lower boundary.

The setup delivered 2.3R.

Expansion SELL → +1.3R

After the original move, the market continued expanding downward.

Several Expansion SELL signals appeared within the same area. We count them as one setup rather than multiple independent trades.

The predefined Take Profit was reached at +1.3R.

Interestingly, the decline continued much further, eventually moving more than 3R from the entry.

But we do not count that additional movement.

The indicator's target was 1.3R, and that is the result recorded in the statistics.

Monday total: +3.6R

Tuesday, November 3

No signals.

Tuesday passed without a qualifying setup.

The indicator did not generate entries simply because the market was moving.

Sometimes the required structure is not there, and that is perfectly normal.

Wednesday, November 4

One Stop-Loss → One successful SELL

Wednesday started with a SELL signal around the opening of the US session.

Unfortunately, the first setup hit its Stop Loss.

But a little later, another SELL signal appeared at a higher price.

This second setup offered a return toward the lower boundary of the pre-market range.

Price followed the expected direction, reached the target and delivered 2R.

First SELL → −1R
Second SELL → +2R

Wednesday total: +1R

Thursday, November 5

A powerful selloff — but only one signal

Thursday was probably the most interesting session of the week.

NASDAQ experienced a powerful downward move, but the Expansion module did not generate an entry during the decline.

Why?

Because the indicator requires specific structural conditions before confirming an Expansion setup.

A strong impulse alone is not enough. The indicator is not designed to chase price after every breakout.

What I particularly liked was that it did not generate a series of premature countertrend signals while the market was falling.

Eventually, it identified one opportunity for a return toward the range.

Return BUY → +1R

Even with a conservative stop placed below the newly formed swing low, the setup offered approximately 1R.

For me, this is a very encouraging observation about how the indicator filters difficult market conditions.

And there is one more interesting detail.

Price moved a considerable distance away from Thursday's range.

I intend to keep that range on my chart next week as an important reference level.

Not because price must return to it, but because previously established boundaries can remain relevant when the market eventually revisits them.

Thursday total: +1R

Friday, November 6

Range SELL → +1.5R

Friday delivered another classic range-sweep setup.

Price swept the upper boundary of the pre-market range, returned inside and confirmed a SELL.

The move continued toward the lower boundary and delivered approximately 1.5R.

But the session did not end there.

Later, several Expansion BUY signals appeared as price attempted to develop a corrective move.

However, Friday evening brought a noticeable decline in volatility.

The market no longer had enough momentum to complete those setups before the weekly close.

So how should we count them?

For now, we don't assign them a profit or a loss.

They remain unresolved, grouped as one Expansion opportunity, and will be evaluated when their actual outcome becomes clear.

And that gives us an interesting question for Monday:

Will the market continue the corrective move after reopening?

Or will the bearish structure remain dominant?

We will see.

Friday completed result: +1.5R

What does this week show us?

Range Sweep and Expansion are two different opportunities.

Monday demonstrated how the original return-inside-range setup and a later Expansion can both reach their predefined targets.

A stop-loss is part of the statistics.

Wednesday's first SELL failed, while the second qualified SELL reached 2R.

We count both signals. There is no reason to ignore a losing setup simply because another one worked.

Not every strong movement should produce an entry.

Thursday showed something especially interesting.

During a powerful selloff, the indicator did not chase the move or repeatedly attempt to catch the bottom.

It waited for its conditions and eventually identified a structured return opportunity with a clear stop-loss level.

We count targets, not the maximum possible movement.

Monday's Expansion SELL eventually had much more room to run.

But its predefined Take Profit was 1.3R.

That is the result we count.

Unfinished signals are not winning signals.

Friday's Expansion BUY setups remain unresolved.

They are recorded separately and will not be added to the completed results until their outcome is known.

What i'm watching next week

Two things have my attention.

First — Thursday's range.

Price moved far away from this area during the selloff. I want to see whether the range becomes relevant again and how price reacts if it returns.

Second — Friday's Expansion BUY signals.

The setups did not complete before the weekend.

Monday's opening price action should provide more information about whether the corrective movement can continue.

Neither scenario is a prediction or a guarantee. They are simply market structures worth monitoring.
