## The week in numbers

This review examines historical indicator setups. R values describe the illustrated entry, stop and exit scenarios, not a record of executed trades.

Another week of reviewing how our NASDAQ Pre-Market Range Sweep indicator performed on the 1-minute chart.

This week brought opportunities to trade back toward the range boundaries and follow some moves further. We also have an addition to the indicator: Expansion signals for continuation beyond the pre-market range.

Wednesday gave us a good example of how this new option works. Let’s go through the week.

### Monday, October 19

One BUY signal, with entry around 29,566.

Price reached the opposite range boundary. After that, we could move the stop to breakeven and let the trade continue.

And Monday gave us a good move: price climbed to approximately 29,819, offering almost 3R.

We did not need to catch the exact high. An exit around 29,770, following the reversal structure or using a trailing stop, would have captured more than 2R.

For this review, we count +2R.

### Tuesday, October 20

Two SELL signals, with entries around 29,450 and 29,443.

Price started moving toward the opposite range boundary shortly after the signals. The nearest resistance around 29,487 provided a reference for the stop-loss.

Both trades reached the opposite boundary at approximately 29,317.

The entries were close together, but their risk/reward ratios were different because they shared the same stop and target.

First SELL → approximately +3.6R.
Second SELL → approximately +2.9R.

Combined result: approximately +6.4R.

The combined result is calculated before rounding each trade.

@img https://s3.tradingview.com/snapshots/f/FbPpfL3r.png

### Wednesday, October 21

No signals on the range-return setup that day.

But now we have another option in the indicator: Expansion.

This signal looks for a continuation setup after price breaks out of the pre-market range.

With Expansion enabled, Wednesday showed a BUY signal above the upper range boundary.

Price reached the Expansion take-profit and continued higher.

We can now look for both types of opportunity: a return toward the range and a continuation beyond it when the conditions are met.

@img https://s3.tradingview.com/snapshots/c/cA4LiSTw.png

We show this Expansion setup separately from the seven signals counted in the main weekly result.

### Thursday, October 22

Two BUY signals.

For the first trade, the stop was placed below the nearest local low. For the second, below the local low formed before that entry.

Both trades reached the opposite range boundary and delivered 1:1 risk/reward.

Neither trade hit its respective stop before reaching the target.

Result: +1R +1R = +2R.

### Friday, October 23

The first SELL signal failed, and the trade hit its stop-loss.

The next SELL appeared above the pre-market range. This time, the target was a retest of the broken upper range boundary.

Entry → 29,077.4.
Stop-loss → 29,174.3, above the high formed during the US session.
1R target → approximately 28,980.5, near the upper range boundary.

The second trade delivered 1R.

Result: −1R +1R = 0R.

## What does this week show us?

The distance to the stop matters just as much as the distance to the target.

Tuesday was a good example. Nearby resistance gave us a clear stop reference, while the opposite range boundary left enough room for a larger reward relative to the risk.

Monday showed another way to manage a trade: reach the opposite boundary, protect the position and follow the move using market structure.

We do not have to catch the exact top to take a good result from a strong move.

And Wednesday showed why we added Expansion.

Sometimes there is no range-return signal, but price breaks out and continues in that direction.

The Expansion option helps us identify those continuation setups. On Wednesday, its BUY signal reached the projected target, and the market continued higher.

The range gives us a reference. The signal gives us a setup. Our risk management determines how we trade it.

Very simple indicator. Very simple strategy.
