Another week of reviewing how our NASDAQ Pre-Market Range Sweep indicator performed on the 1-minute chart.

This week, we added something new — Expansion BUY and SELL signals.

Our original signals look for a move back into the pre-market range after a sweep. But price doesn't always return to the midpoint or the opposite boundary.

Sometimes, it continues moving away from the range.

The new signals help us follow that scenario:

Range breakout → Pullback and boundary retest → Break of the setup high or low → Expansion signal.

The target calculation uses the range width and the entry’s distance from the broken boundary.

Let's see how both types of signals worked this week.

### Monday, October 12

The first signal was our new Expansion SELL at approximately 30,734.

Its target projected an equal distance beyond the broken lower boundary. Price eventually reached it, although this happened later, during the night.

We count +1R for this setup.

While that move was developing, the original BUY signal offered a trade in the opposite direction — a rebound toward the range.

That BUY delivered 1R and slightly more, but price never reached the lower range boundary.

This is exactly why we shouldn't assume that every rebound will bring price back into the range.

A continuation move can include pullbacks. Both setups can appear on the same day.

Result: +2R — two setups, +1R each.

Price broke below the range, but the original BUY signal did not appear.

## What does this week show us?

A sweep doesn't guarantee a full return into the range.

Monday showed this clearly. The BUY offered a profitable rebound, while the broader move continued lower.

The two signal types help us follow two different scenarios:

Original BUY and SELL → a move back toward or into the range.

Expansion BUY and SELL → continuation beyond the range after a retest and a break of the setup extreme.

The original signals can give us a short stop behind a nearby local high or low. Expansion helps us follow a move that continues beyond the range, rather than waiting indefinitely for price to return.

We still need to define our stop, calculate our position size and decide where to take profit. An Expansion target is a reference, not a promise.

The range gives us the reference levels. The setup gives us a reason to act.

Very simple indicator. Two clear scenarios.
